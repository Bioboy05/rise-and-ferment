## Top 7 Creator Methods

### 1. Joshua Weissman
- **Days:** 7 (up to 10 in cold climates)
- **Flour:** Day 1 rye flour, then all-purpose flour from Day 2 onward
- **Hydration:** ~100% (Day 1 is 150%: 100g rye + 150g water)
- **Feeding:** Once daily Days 1-4, twice daily Days 5-6, ready Day 7
- **Time between feedings:** 24h then 12h
- **Visual cues:** Bubbles after Day 1; sourdough smell Day 3; Day 4 may look dead (don't panic); doubles in 4-5h by Day 5; nearly triples by Day 7
- **Temperature:** 25-28°C recommended
- **Unique:** Entertainment-focused YouTube presentation, starts with rye Day 1 only then switches to AP

### 2. Tartine Bakery / Chad Robertson
- **Days:** 14-21 (longest of all methods)
- **Flour:** 50/50 blend of white bread flour and whole wheat flour throughout
- **Hydration:** 100%
- **Feeding:** Once daily, discard 80% each time
- **Time between feedings:** 24h
- **Visual cues:** Bubbling/puffing in first 2-3 days; ready when rises and falls predictably; uses float test
- **Unique:** Longest process; prefers "young" leaven with sweet/nutty flavor; leaven concept (400g, use 200g, 200g becomes next starter)

### 3. King Arthur Baking
- **Days:** 5-7 (most optimistic timeline)
- **Flour:** Day 1 whole wheat or pumpernickel; Days 2+ all-purpose
- **Hydration:** 100% (50g flour + 50g water)
- **Feeding:** Once daily Days 1-2, twice daily Days 3-5 (~12h apart)
- **Time between feedings:** 24h then ~12h
- **Visual cues:** Day 3 bubbling + fruity aroma. Day 5 readiness: doubles in 6-8h. Hooch (brown liquid) = hungry. Pink/orange = contaminated.
- **Unique:** Most optimistic timeline; industry-leading troubleshooting docs; sells dried starter; baker's hotline support

### 4. The Perfect Loaf (Maurizio Leo)
- **Days:** 7 (7 steps)
- **Flour:** Mix of whole grain rye + white flour, continuous rye throughout
- **Hydration:** 100%
- **Feeding ratio:** 1:5:5 (20% ripe starter, 100% water, 100% flour)
- **Feeding:** Twice daily at 12-hour intervals from the start
- **Time between feedings:** 12h consistently
- **Visual cues:** Rise, bubbles on top and sides, sour aroma, consistent daily rise pattern
- **Unique:** Most scientific and detailed method; James Beard Award; software engineer background; Discord community; 72-78°F / 22-25°C

### 5. Foodgeek (Sune Trudslev)
- **Days:** 6-7
- **Flour:** Day 1 rye flour; Days 2+ bread flour
- **Hydration:** Day 1 ~150%; Days 2+ 100%
- **Feeding:** Once daily
- **Time between feedings:** 24h
- **Visual cues:** Active = doubles in 4-8h after feeding; brownish liquid = normal; mold = discard
- **Unique:** 60+ videos with controlled experiments; pioneered timelapse recordings of starter growth; data-driven methodology; 25-28°C

### 6. Pro Home Cooks (Mike Greenfield)
- **Days:** ~7
- **Flour:** All-purpose flour ONLY (simplest)
- **Hydration:** ~100%
- **Feeding:** Once daily Days 1-3, twice daily Day 4+
- **Time between feedings:** 24h then 12h
- **Visual cues:** Small bubbles after 24h; bubbly and active by end of week
- **Unique:** Most accessible/simplified; AP flour only; $147 Sourdough U course; cooler temp (20-22°C)

### 7. Full Proof Baking (Kristen Dennis)
- **Days:** ~7
- **Flour:** 10% Arrowhead Mills organic rye + 90% King Arthur bread flour
- **Hydration:** 100%
- **Feeding:** Daily
- **Unique:** PhD in Biology; named starter "Ozzy" (since 2016); sells physical kits ($44), jars ($30), live starters on Amazon; temperature strips on jars; aliquot jar method

## Universal Consensus

All 7 creators agree on:
1. **100% hydration** — universal standard
2. **~7 days** — typical timeline (5 of 7)
3. **Rye flour for initial days** — jump-starts fermentation (5 of 7)
4. **Temperature 21-28°C** — the sweet spot
5. **Doubling in 4-8 hours** — readiness indicator
6. **Once daily → twice daily** — feeding progression
7. **Discard and feed** — universal practice

## Top 10 Beginner Mistakes (by frequency)

1. **Using starter too early** — Day 2-3 bacterial bloom ≠ ready yeast
2. **Not measuring feedings** — eyeballing leads to inconsistency
3. **Wrong temperature** — too cold = sluggish, too hot = over-ferments
4. **Chlorinated water** — disrupts yeast development (use filtered)
5. **Over-discarding** — weakens the culture
6. **Panicking at Day 3-4 "dead zone"** — #1 psychological dropout point
7. **Adding accelerants** — raisins, pineapple juice don't speed things up
8. **Changing flour types mid-process** — starter stalls, takes 1-2 weeks to adapt
9. **Airtight containers** — need gas exchange
10. **Metal containers/utensils** — commonly warned against

## Multiple Starters — Evidence

People DO maintain multiple starters for:
1. Different flavor profiles (whole wheat, rye, white — each develops distinct flavors)
2. Different fermentation characteristics (rye is more active/forgiving)
3. Different bread types (rye starter for rye bread, wheat for white)
4. Backup insurance (contamination protection)
5. Hobby/collection (named starters, regional, inherited/vintage)

**BUT** most experts recommend ONE starter + different levain builds. The multi-starter behavior is real but represents ~5% of users.
