## Email 2 — Day 3: First Signs of Life

**Subject:** Day 3 — is your starter doing something weird?
**Preview text:** If it smells terrible, you're on track.

---

Hey {name|there},

If you started your starter when you got this guide, today is around Day 3. And if your kitchen smells like nail polish remover or old cheese right now — congratulations. That's exactly what should be happening.

**Here's what's going on:**

Around Day 3, bacteria called leuconostoc throw a little party in your jar. They create bubbles, weird smells, and sometimes a dramatic rise. It looks like your starter is ready.

It's not. This is the "false rise." These bacteria will die off in the next day or two, replaced by the lactobacillus that you actually want. When the activity drops tomorrow, it will feel like your starter died.

**It didn't die. It's evolving.**

This is the exact moment when most people give up. Don't be most people.

Three things to do today:
1. Feed as usual (discard half, add 50g flour + 50g water)
2. Mark the level with a rubber band
3. Take a deep breath and trust the process

I killed my first starter on Day 3 because I panicked. My second one became the best thing that ever happened to my kitchen.

Talk soon — Day 5 is when things start getting interesting.

— Rise & Ferment

## Email 3 — Day 5: The Quiet Middle

**Subject:** Day 5 — the silence before the bread
**Preview text:** Your starter isn't dead. Here's proof.

---

Hey {name|there},

Day 5 is... quiet. After the drama of Day 3, your starter might look completely dead. No bubbles. No rise. Nothing.

I remember staring at mine thinking "I've been feeding flour to a jar of paste for five days."

**But here's what's happening that you can't see:**

The good bacteria (lactobacillus) are moving in. They're creating an acidic environment that kills off the early organisms. Think of it like a renovation — things look worse before they get better.

**Quick check — look for these signs:**
- Tiny bubbles trapped in the mixture (not just on top)
- The smell shifting from "chemical" to "yogurt-like"
- Even a slight tang when you lean in close

If you see ANY of these, your starter is alive and building strength.

**The #1 thing that slows starters down:**

Cold kitchens. If your home is below 20°C, your starter is basically trying to run a marathon in a freezer. The fix is simple:

🔹 **Oven light trick:** Oven OFF, light ON, starter inside. That little bulb keeps it at ~25°C — perfect.
🔹 **Near a radiator:** Not ON it — near it. 30cm away with a towel between.
🔹 **Warm water bath:** Jar nestled in a container of warm water.

I wrote a whole article about temperature and why it's the #1 reason bread fails: https://riseandferment.com (click Blog → "Why Your Bread Won't Rise")

Two more days. You're closer than you think.

— Rise & Ferment

**Preview text:** What happens after the first 14 days (and a gift)

Hey {name|there},

If you're still here, feeding your starter, watching it grow — you've done something most people don't. You chose to slow down and create something with your own hands. That's special. And it doesn't stop here.

**What happens next:**

Your starter will keep getting better. A 3-month-old starter is noticeably stronger than a 2-week-old one. The flavour deepens, the rise becomes more predictable, and the whole process becomes second nature.

**A few tips for the long game:**

1. **Fridge storage:** Once your starter is strong, you can keep it in the fridge and feed it just once a week. Pull it out the night before you want to bake.

2. **Name it.** I'm serious. People who name their starters take better care of them. (Mine is called Bubbles. No judgement.)

3. **Track your bakes.** Note your times, temperatures, and what worked. Patterns emerge that make you a better baker.

We built a free app for exactly this — tracking feedings, temperatures, streaks, and progress. It even has a day-by-day guide built in: https://riseandferment.com/app

**Your first bread will be ugly. And delicious.**

Don't aim for Instagram-worthy on loaf #1. Aim for "I made this with my own hands and it tastes amazing." The pretty scores and open crumbs come with practice.

If you need any help along the way, our blog has honest, no-gatekeeping guides for everything from troubleshooting to recipes: https://riseandferment.com
