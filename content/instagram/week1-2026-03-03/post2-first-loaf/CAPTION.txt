The first loaf is never perfect. And that's exactly the point.

Mine was dense, slightly gummy on the inside, and the scoring looked like a toddler attacked it with a razor blade. I took one look and thought: "Well, that's embarrassing."

Then I tore off a piece. Still warm. Crunchy crust. Tangy crumb. I'd made it. With my own hands. From something I'd been feeding in a jar for two weeks.

It wasn't Instagram-worthy. But it was the most satisfying thing I'd eaten in years.

Here's what I want you to know: the first loaf doesn't have to be beautiful. It has to exist. Everything after that is just practice.

If you're holding a lumpy, lopsided, slightly-too-dark sourdough right now — congratulations. You did it. You're a baker.

Now make the second one.
